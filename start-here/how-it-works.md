# How this course works

## The weekly rhythm

Each week is one page with seven tabs. Work through them in order; they are designed as a loop of *motivate → explain → discuss → do → check → extend → improve*.

| Tab | What it is for | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Start here** | Why it matters, objectives, key terms, a 5-question pre-check | 20 min |
| **2 · Concepts** | The ideas, layered from intuition to detail, with worked numbers | 90-120 min |
| **3 · Conversation** | A dialogue between Zainab, Tunde and Dr. Mensah that tackles the hard ideas - read it *after* Concepts and argue back | 20 min |
| **4 · Hands-on lab** | Step-by-step practice with real tools on the course data | 3-4 h |
| **5 · Practice** | Graded exercises, a self-test and a portfolio piece | 60-90 min |
| **6 · Go deeper** | Annotated reading, courses and tools - pick what suits you | optional |
| **7 · Contribute** | Ways to improve this page - the course is yours too | optional |

**Total:** about 6-8 hours a week for 12 weeks. If you have less time, double the calendar: a 24-week pace works well alongside a full-time job.

## Three ways to study

| Mode | How | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-paced** | Follow the pages alone; check answers in the folded sections | Busy professionals |
| **Study group** | 4-8 people meet weekly (45-60 min) to discuss the *talking points* and compare lab results | Institutions, journal clubs |
| **Facilitated cohort** | A tutor runs live help sessions and marks the capstone | Training programmes |

## Rules for the labs

1. **Always work from the repository root** (the folder containing `README.md`).
2. **Never edit files in `data/`.** Write all your outputs to `results/weekNN/` (it is git-ignored and safe to delete).
3. **Type the commands** instead of pasting them for the first few weeks; typing builds the muscle memory. Paste when you are tired.
4. **Compare your output with "What you should see".** If it differs, do not move on: use the troubleshooting table and the [lab-problem form](../contribute/README.md).
5. **Do not open `data/**/answers/` until the "check your answer" step.** The learning is in the struggle.
6. **Keep a lab notebook** (a plain text file `results/notebook.md`): what you ran, what surprised you, what you still do not understand.

## Assessment (for cohorts; self-learners can self-assess)

| Component | Weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly self-tests and portfolio pieces (Weeks 1-11) | 40% | Completion plus quality of the portfolio piece |
| Participation (discussion threads, helping a peer, improving a page) | 10% | One accepted contribution counts fully |
| Capstone project (Week 12) | 40% | Rubric in Week 12 |
| Peer review of a classmate's capstone | 10% | Structured review form in Week 12 |

## Getting help

1. Re-read the **Key terms** and the **Concepts** tab section that matches your problem.
2. Search the [glossary](glossary.md).
3. Ask in **GitHub Discussions** (questions) - include the command you ran, the full message, and the output of `bash scripts/check_setup.sh`.
4. If the page is wrong or unclear, open an **issue** - that is a contribution, not a complaint.

## A note on honesty about what you will learn

Public health genomics is a large field. In twelve weeks you will not become a clinical geneticist or a senior bioinformatician. You *will* gain a working vocabulary, the ability to run and critique standard analyses on small data, the habits of reproducible work, and a map of where to go next. That is exactly the foundation experts will build on when they extend this course.
