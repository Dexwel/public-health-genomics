# Public Health Genomics: an open 12-week course

**Learn to read, analyse and responsibly use genomic data to improve the health of populations** - from tracing an outbreak to appraising a screening programme - even if you have never opened a terminal.

{% hint style="success" %}
**This course is a starting point.** Experts are invited to correct, extend, localise and translate it. It is designed so that a single typo fix or a whole new case study can be merged safely. See [How to contribute](contribute/README.md).
{% endhint %}

## What you will be able to do

By Week 12 you will be able to:

1. Explain the scope, evidence standards and ethics of public health genomics (human and pathogen).
2. Work confidently on the **Linux command line** and in **R**, with reproducible scripts under version control.
3. Take sequencing reads through **quality control, alignment, variant calling and annotation**, and judge which findings to trust.
4. Run a **genome-wide association study** and a **polygenic risk score**, spot population-structure traps, and discuss equity and portability.
5. Analyse **outbreak genomes**: SNP distances, trees, clusters, and resistance markers; write a situation report.
6. Plan genomic programmes with attention to **consent, data governance, implementation and evaluation**, particularly in African and other low- and middle-income settings.
7. Complete and peer-review a **capstone mini-project**.

## How the course works

- **12 weeks, about 6-8 hours per week.** Each week is one page with **seven tabs**: *Start here · Concepts · Conversation · Hands-on lab · Practice · Go deeper · Contribute*.
- **Conversation, not just text.** Three fictional colleagues - Zainab (a district health officer), Tunde (a lab scientist) and Dr. Mensah (their mentor) - argue through the hard ideas so you hear the questions you were afraid to ask.
- **Hands-on from Day 1.** Every lab uses small **synthetic datasets** that run on a laptop in minutes and come with answer keys, so you can check your own work. No real patient data are used.
- **Linux and R are taught from scratch** in Weeks 2 and 3, because everything afterwards depends on them.

## Who this is for

Public health practitioners, epidemiologists, laboratory staff, clinicians, data analysts, policy makers and students. You need basic epidemiology and statistics (proportions, odds ratios, p-values). **No programming or genomics background is assumed.**
